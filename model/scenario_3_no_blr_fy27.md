# Scenario 3 — No BLR Licence in FY27

**BLR revenue window in FY27: 0 months.** Downside / stress case.

## FY27 P&L (₹ Cr)

| Line | Amount | Note |
|------|-------:|------|
| Interest Income (FD) | 1.50 | actual-based |
| Existing operating Sales | 0.97 | actual-based |
| BLR revenue | 0.00 | no licence this year |
| **Total income** | **2.47** | |
| Total expense | (5.34) | revised bottom-up, incl. new hires, RC fees, board meetings, IT/branding — see `01_monthly_budget_fy27.md` |
| **Net P&L** | **−2.87 Cr** | full-year operating loss |

## Runway / solvency
- Net cash burn ≈ **₹23-24 L/month** (revised expense base is ~7 L/yr higher; burn moves marginally).
- Drawable liquidity (ex the likely-locked ₹25 Cr net-worth corpus) ≈ ₹1.9 Cr → **~8 months** before the corpus is touched.
- Company remains **solvent** on the ₹26.1 Cr FD base, but burns ~₹2.87 Cr of value in the year with no BLR offset.

## Decisions this scenario forces
- **Cost discipline:** with no BLR revenue, the ₹95 L/yr of new hires is the largest discretionary commitment. Board should decide:
  - [ ] Proceed with both hires regardless (bet on BLR landing early FY28)?
  - [ ] Stagger / defer Hire B if licence slips?
- Push existing operating Sales harder (it grew from ₹8 L in FY26 to ₹28.5 L in Q1 FY27 — momentum exists).
- Re-underwrite FD laddering to maximise interest income while corpus is idle.

## Narrative for board
- This is the **planning floor** — if BLR does not arrive, the business runs a ~₹2.8 Cr loss funded from reserves.
- Not an existential risk given the corpus, but it is a year of **capital erosion**, and makes the case for tight cost control until BLR is secured.

> This scenario has no BLR `TBD` inputs — it is fully derived from actuals + confirmed new-hire costs.
