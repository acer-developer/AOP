The constraint and what it costs

Capacity is the real swing factor

Leaders move out of case execution into RC and review only. That leaves five executors to carry both books. One case is one person-week, so real throughput is about 20 cases a month, total, across IPO and BLR.

Salary expense, now and from September

Salary runs at ₹25.0 L a month today, rising to ₹32.92 L (25.0 plus 7.92) from September once fully staffed.

Salary = salary + PF + gratuity + ESI + EDLI, per booked actuals. Run-rate from September ₹32.92 L/mo, about ₹3.95 Cr/yr.

How 20 cases a month get split

IPO ramps to 20 mandates by Oct. That alone can fill the team, which is exactly why the BLR start has to be paced.

The playbook, three ways

Pick when BLR lands, the whole plan follows

Same team, same IPO book, same cost base. Only the BLR licence date changes, and with it how we pace the launch so IPO delivery never breaks.

Execution is the multiplier. Every number here assumes IPO and rating cases are delivered on time and to quality. Handle the case flow well and the same team compounds into these figures. Slip on execution, miss deadlines or let quality drop, and the story changes badly: revenue stalls while the full cost base keeps running, and even the BLR scenarios stop reaching break-even. IPO and ratings, done right, is what carries every scenario.

    The numbers behind the plan

    Revenue mix and cost composition

    Every figure here is built from booked actuals or the confirmed unit economics: IPO at ₹1.2 L per quarter per mandate, BLR at 2.5 bps up-front plus 1.5 bps recurring on a ₹100 Cr average case.

    Revenue mix at the Apr27 run-rate (S1)

    Annualising the April 2027 exit month: BLR leads, IPO and ratings is a steady annuity, FD interest becomes the smaller slice.

    ACER has only two kinds of income: rating fees and FD interest. Rating splits into IPO and ratings (existing work plus the growing IPO book) and BLR once the licence is live. BLR is a labelled capacity projection, not booked. IPO and ratings about ₹1.45 Cr/yr, FD interest held flat.

    Where the ₹5.34 Cr cost base goes

    Employee expense still dominates. The new lines, RC, secretarial, IT and the branding buffer, are the smaller wedges.

    FY27 cost base rebuilt bottom-up. Consultant per-case fee excluded pending confirmation, insurance dropped.

    The one decision that de-risks everything

    Phase IPO delivery with clients now, before the licence lands. If all 20 IPO mandates pile into October, they eat the whole team the same month BLR needs to launch. A committed Oct, Nov, Dec delivery calendar is what lets BLR start on time. This is the single highest-leverage move in the plan.

    When to add the sixth executor

    Not on a hunch. Hire the moment residual capacity, team capacity minus IPO load minus BLR load, goes negative for two straight months, and only in the BLR-live scenarios. In the no-BLR case, hold, there is neither the case volume nor the cash cushion to justify it.

    Good to have, kept out of the base plan

    Deliberately excluded from every number above so the plan stays honest on committed cost. Fund these as profitability allows, once BLR revenue is real.

    Insurance

    Group health and office cover, dropped from the base case earlier. Add back once the loss narrows.

    Festival celebration & gifts

    Team festival spend and client gifting. Small, morale and relationship building, but discretionary.

    Employee appraisal

    Annual increments and performance rewards. Not in the flat salary run-rate; budget once revenue supports it.
