# ACER vs Peers — Reality Check for the Board

> *Is what ACER is planning (get the BLR/RBI-ECAI licence, add two senior hires,
> run a ₹5.27 Cr cost base) acceptable and realistic against how the Indian rating
> industry actually makes money?*
>
> ACER figures = Zoho Books actuals (see [`../data/actuals/`](../data/actuals/)).
> Peer figures = verified filings (see [`../research/cra_peer_financials.md`](../research/cra_peer_financials.md)).

---

## 1. Where ACER sits on the industry map

| | Operating (rating) revenue | Stage |
|--|---------------------------:|-------|
| CRISIL (Ratings segment) | ₹909 Cr | Market leader (~60% share) |
| CARE (Ratings segment) | ₹360 Cr | #2/#3 |
| India Ratings | ₹233 Cr | Fitch-backed |
| Infomerics | ₹91 Cr | Small, BLR-led |
| Acuité | ~₹55 Cr | Smallest established |
| **ACER** | **~₹0.9–1.0 Cr run-rate** (FY27 Q1 Sales ₹28.5 L annualised; FY26 was just ₹8 L) | **Inception** |

**ACER's operating revenue is ~2% of the smallest established peer (Acuité).** This is not a criticism — it is simply the true starting point: ACER is a *brand-new* CRA (SEBI-registered 2025) that has not yet switched on its main revenue engine.

---

## 2. The one ratio the board must internalise

| Metric | Profitable peers | ACER (now) |
|--------|-----------------:|-----------:|
| Employee cost as % of **revenue** | 43–54% | **~360%** (payroll ~₹3.6 Cr vs operating revenue ~₹1 Cr) |
| Operating margin | +27% to +56% | deeply negative (FY27 tracking ~−₹2.8 Cr) |

- A mature CRA spends ~half its revenue on people **and still makes 27–56% margins**, because revenue sits far above the fixed people/compliance cost.
- ACER has built (and is expanding) a people cost base **sized like a ₹6–8 Cr-revenue CRA, while earning ~₹1 Cr.** That is the entire financial tension in this AOP.
- **This is only rational as a deliberate, pre-revenue investment** — hiring ahead of the BLR licence so capacity exists to service mandates the moment RBI-ECAI accreditation lands.

---

## 3. Is the strategy right? — YES, and the industry proves it

1. **BLR is the correct engine.** Every small Indian CRA (Infomerics ₹91 Cr, Acuité ₹55 Cr, ex-Brickwork) is **bank-loan-rating-led** — because any bank borrower is a candidate, giving a far larger pool than bonds. ACER betting its growth on the BLR/RBI-ECAI licence is exactly how comparable CRAs scaled. See [`../research/industry_and_regulation.md`](../research/industry_and_regulation.md).
2. **The hardest barrier is already cleared.** The SEBI **₹25 Cr net-worth** requirement — which sits as ACER's Bank of India FD — is a real moat most would-be entrants can't meet. ACER has it *and* earns ~₹1.5 Cr/yr of FD interest that cushions the burn.
3. **Recurring economics are attractive.** Issuer-pays + **annual surveillance fees** means each rating won becomes a compounding annuity — revenue quality improves as the book builds.

**Verdict on strategy: acceptable and well-aligned with the industry.**

---

## 4. Is the *cost timing* acceptable? — CONDITIONAL

The risk is not the strategy; it is the **gap between committing cost and earning revenue**:

- The two senior hires add **₹95 L/yr** (₹64 L in FY27), lifting the cost base to **₹5.27 Cr** against ~₹1 Cr operating revenue.
- **Break-even needs ~₹5.3 Cr of revenue** — roughly a **5–6× increase** from today. The peer map shows that is *achievable for a BLR-led small CRA* (Infomerics/Acuité prove it), but it is a **multi-year ramp**, not an FY27 event.

Mapping to the three scenarios ([`../model/scenario_comparison.md`](../model/scenario_comparison.md)):

| Scenario | Is the ₹95 L hire bet acceptable? |
|----------|-----------------------------------|
| **S1 — BLR by Oct** | **Yes.** 6 months of BLR revenue + capacity to service it justifies hiring now. Best alignment of cost and revenue. |
| **S2 — BLR by Jan** | **Conditional.** Team on payroll ~8 months but BLR revenue only in Q4 → widest cost/revenue gap. Consider staggering the second hire if the licence looks like slipping. |
| **S3 — No BLR in FY27** | **Hard to justify on FY27 economics alone.** ~₹2.8 Cr loss with the full team and zero BLR. Acceptable *only* as an explicit "invest through the trough" board decision, with cost-discipline triggers. |

---

## 5. Bottom line for the board

- **What ACER is doing is acceptable and strategically sound** — it mirrors exactly how every comparable small CRA built its business (BLR-led, issuer-pays, surveillance annuity), and ACER has already cleared the ₹25 Cr net-worth barrier.
- **FY27 is unavoidably an investment year.** With operating revenue at ~₹1 Cr and a ₹5.27 Cr cost base, ACER will run a loss funded by FD interest and reserves regardless of scenario. That is normal for a CRA at this stage — *provided the BLR licence lands*.
- **The single most important variable is the BLR/RBI-ECAI licence.** Everything the board approves (especially the ₹95 L of hires) should be explicitly framed as *"conditional on BLR timing."*
- **Three guardrails to set now:**
  1. A **cost-discipline trigger** if BLR slips past Jan (Scenario 3 path) — e.g. stagger the second hire, freeze discretionary spend.
  2. A **revenue-ramp milestone plan** toward ~₹5 Cr (the break-even line), benchmarked against Infomerics/Acuité's early trajectory.
  3. A **rating-quality / compliance gate that scales with BLR volume.** The Brickwork case is the warning: a top-tier CRA whose SEBI registration was cancelled in 2022 over rating-process and disclosure lapses, and whose revenue then **collapsed to ~₹10.9 Cr**. As ACER pushes BLR volume, process discipline is existential — franchise value can be destroyed by one regulatory action far faster than it is built. See [`../research/cra_peer_financials.md`](../research/cra_peer_financials.md).
- **Solvency is not in question** (₹26 Cr corpus); **operational self-sufficiency is the goal**, and BLR is the only credible path to it.
