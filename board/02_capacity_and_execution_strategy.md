# Capacity & Execution Strategy — FY27 AOP

*How to actually make the FY27 plan happen: BLR, IPO, and Ratings on one team, under
three licence-timing scenarios.*

## 0. The core problem in one line

**The three scenarios (S1/S2/S3) model *when* BLR revenue can start. They do not yet
model *whether the team has free hands to execute it* when it does.** Licence timing
is a regulatory risk you don't control. Capacity is an execution risk you do control —
and it is currently the bigger threat to Scenario 1's Dec break-even than the licence
itself.

## 1. Why capacity broke this year

| Change | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Leaders move out of case execution (RC/review only) | Case-execution headcount drops from 7 to **5 executors** |
| IPO track uses the same team | 10 IPOs now → **~20 by Oct** — doubles in ~3 months |
| RC meetings draw executors away from casework | 10-12 meetings/month × 3 executors/meeting = real scheduling load on top of the 1-case-per-person-week execution time |
| Stated "real capacity ~20/month" | This is the *ceiling*, not a number with headroom built in |

**The math that matters:** if IPO demand alone reaches ~20/month by October, and total
team capacity is ~20/month, **there is zero residual capacity for BLR cases in the
exact month Scenario 1 needs BLR execution to begin.** A licence landing on 1-Oct is a
regulatory event, not a revenue event — revenue only lands if a case gets worked.

### Illustrative capacity model (Jul → Dec, person-cases/month)

| Month | Team capacity | IPO demand (10→20 linear ramp to Oct, held flat after) | Residual for BLR/Ratings |
|-------|---------------:|---------------------------------------------------------:|--------------------------:|
| Jul (now) | 20 | 10 | 10 |
| Aug | 20 | ~13 | ~7 |
| Sep | 20 | ~17 | ~3 |
| Oct | 20 | 20 | **0** |
| Nov | 20 | 20 | **0** |
| Dec | 20 | 20 | **0** |

*(Illustrative only — built from the "10 now → ~20 by Oct" figure and the stated
20/month capacity ceiling. Needs the real IPO ramp curve from BD to replace the linear
assumption, but the shape of the problem won't change: residual capacity heads to zero
right as BLR would need it most.)*

**This is exactly why open item #1 — confirming the phased Oct/Nov/Dec IPO
delivery with clients — is the highest-leverage decision on the table.** It isn't a
scheduling nicety; it's what determines whether Scenario 1 is real or just a date on
a calendar.

## 2. The fix: treat capacity as an allocation decision, not a hope

Three levers, in order of how fast they help:

1. **Phase IPO delivery to clients now, explicitly.** Don't let "20 by Oct" be an
   unstated ceiling everyone discovers in October. Tell clients up front: X IPOs
   close in Oct, Y in Nov, Z in Dec — a real queue, not a pile-up. This is the
   single biggest thing that protects BLR execution capacity without costing a
   client relationship (a committed date beats a silent slip).
2. **Give BLR first claim on freed capacity the moment the licence lands**, in
   whichever scenario. Pre-decide this now so it isn't negotiated in a panic in
   Oct/Jan: once BLR is live, new IPO intake queues behind BLR mandates, not the
   other way round.
3. **Hire the 6th executor when residual capacity goes negative for two
   consecutive months** — not before (no case volume to justify it yet) and not
   after (by then you've already turned away or delayed revenue-generating work).
   See §5.

## 3. Scenario-by-scenario playbook

### Scenario 1 — BLR by Oct (best case)
- **Real risk is capacity, not licence.** Per §1, Oct residual capacity is ~0 under
  the current IPO ramp. Without phasing IPO intake, S1's "break-even by Dec" is at
  risk of becoming "licence live by Oct, first BLR case worked in Dec" — a 2-month
  silent slip nobody planned for.
- **Action:** lock the IPO phasing conversation with clients in August, before the
  licence lands, so BLR has a lane the day it's live.
- **Cost base:** ₹5.34 Cr (revised). Needs ~₹47.8 L/month of BLR revenue over 6
  months to fully close the gap — steep, so expect a narrowed loss, not full
  profitability, even in the best case.

### Scenario 2 — BLR by Jan (middle case)
- More runway to fix capacity before it matters (5 more months to phase IPO
  delivery and build the queue discipline from §2).
- **Key tension unchanged:** new hires are on payroll ~8 months before BLR revenue
  arrives in the last 3. Use the extra lead time to push existing operating Sales
  harder — it's the only lever that improves the picture before BLR arrives.
- **Cost base:** ₹5.34 Cr. Needs ~₹95.7 L/month of BLR revenue over 3 months —
  very steep; treat FY27 as a loss year in this scenario and focus on FY28 setup.

### Scenario 3 — No BLR in FY27 (downside)
- This is a **preserve-optionality year**, not a growth year. Don't chase 20+ IPOs
  just because demand exists — deliberately throttle intake to what 5 executors can
  sustainably carry without burning out the team that needs to sprint the moment
  BLR does land.
- **Do not hire the 6th executor in this branch** — no case volume or cash cushion
  justifies it.
- **Cost base:** ₹5.34 Cr against ₹2.47 Cr locked-in income → **−₹2.87 Cr** loss,
  funded from the FD corpus. Solvent, not existential, but real capital erosion —
  hold cost discipline (this is where the IT/branding one-time spend and RC-fee
  discretion matter most; don't let discretionary spend drift up in a loss year).

## 4. RC meetings — cost and capacity, together

- ₹20,000/meeting × 10-12 meetings/month ≈ ₹2.0-2.4 L/month cash cost.
- Each meeting draws **3 of the 5 executors** away from casework for its duration.
  Batching 2 cases per meeting where possible reduces both the cash cost and the
  capacity drag — worth making a standing instruction to the RC coordinator rather
  than an occasional efficiency.

## 5. Analyst hire — decision rule, not a debate

Hire the 6th executor **the first month residual capacity (team capacity − IPO
demand − BLR demand) goes negative for two consecutive months**, and only in S1 or
S2. In S3, don't hire — see §3.

## 6. Board governance cadence

Three board meetings remain in FY27 (Aug-1, ~Nov, ~Feb — see
`../model/01_monthly_budget_fy27.md` §4). Each one should carry the same three lines,
not just a licence-status update:

1. BLR licence status
2. Residual capacity (team capacity − IPO demand − BLR demand), by month
3. Burn rate against the FY27 cost base and the FD corpus

This is the combination that actually determines whether the scenario you're in is
survivable — tracking licence status alone (as the model did before this pass) misses
half the risk.

## 7. Open items, ranked by leverage

1. **Confirm phased Oct/Nov/Dec IPO delivery with clients** — highest leverage;
   determines whether S1 is real (§1-2).
2. **Confirm consultant fee per case** — needed to close the variable-cost side of
   the model (currently excluded, not zero).
3. **Confirm exact board sitting-fee rate** — currently proxied at ₹5.5 L/meeting.
4. **Set the analyst-hire trigger formally** — rule is drafted in §5; needs board
   sign-off so it isn't a live debate when the trigger fires.
